# Notty

Native iPad notebooks for handwriting, annotated PDFs, and lecture review.

Notty uses SwiftUI for navigation and PencilKit inside a UIKit editor. Paper is one continuous surface with subtle A4 boundaries for future page-wise export. The primary device is the 12.9-inch iPad Pro, fifth generation, running iPadOS 27.0.1 with Apple Pencil.

## Current version

The writing study contains native PencilKit input, finger scrolling, pinch zoom, an app-owned drawing toolbar, and continuous paper that can grow from three to twelve A4 sections. The minimum zoom can show a complete section; dividers and the paper edge follow scrolling through layer transforms. Paper can extend manually or after an eligible completed drawing change leaves ink near the last section’s end. It saves and reopens the selected notebook. A failed opening offers an explicit new-notebook action that preserves the original notebook and its recovery copies. Save failures distinguish content limits, changed revisions, damaged or unsupported files, and unavailable storage. Pending edits retain their original order and timestamps.

Writing pauses while saves catch up or a failure needs attention. Brief capacity pauses keep the toolbar, focus and viewport stable. Undo and Redo keys use guarded native commands; failed, read-only and recovery states block them. Save feedback is a readout in the notebook's header, with details and recovery actions in a popover. Saving appears after a short delay, and successful recovery feedback expires. Opening recovery copies waits until the native tool is inactive and suspends toolbar access. Reading, scrolling and zoom remain available. Errors explain the available actions; retry is offered when the underlying condition can recover.

A header floats over the top of the page: the "notty" nameplate, a recessed tray with the note title and its limit, history and save readouts, Undo, Redo and an accent key that opens the notebook menu. The menu chooses the writing view and the toolbar position, adds a page, opens recovery copies, takes over Redo when the header is too narrow for its key, and lists the features coming later. Three writing views decide when the header and tools leave the page. Focus, the default, hides both when a stroke ends and leaves a small reveal tab, or a handle for a floating toolbar; tapping it, scrolling up about 28 points, reaching the top of the notebook, a status-bar tap, ⌥⌘T or Escape brings them back, and an Apple Pencil tool switch shows the tools briefly. Pinned keeps both in place. Always-on tools keeps the toolbar over the page while the header scrolls away with it. In every view the header and tools stay in sight while writing is paused, a save, recovery or history problem needs attention, a popover or sheet is open, the toolbar is dragged, keyboard focus is in them, or VoiceOver or Switch Control is running. The writing view is stored with the toolbar preferences and can also be chosen with ⌃⌘1, ⌃⌘2 and ⌃⌘3. Focus keeps the status bar and lets the home indicator fade over the page.

The toolbar overlays the page in every position, with top-left, centre and right anchors below the header or a freely floating position where it can collapse to a small handle; it never moves into the header's place. The canvas fills the window behind the header, status bar and home indicator; the paper scrolls behind every floating control, with extra edge scroll range to reveal covered writing. The space the header and toolbar cover stays reserved whether or not they show, so the page never moves when they come and go. Dragging the toolbar near a dock snaps it into place. Tool rows scroll horizontally inside the toolbar. It offers PencilKit's native inks with fine, medium and bold widths, quick and recent colour swatches, a colour picker, object and pixel erasers, the lasso and the ruler. Proposed presets set an ink, colour and width together; their values are starting points for review, not final choices. Apple Pencil double-tap and squeeze follow the system setting: they switch to the eraser or the previous tool, and when the setting would show Apple's palette they show the toolbar's colours instead. A collapsed toolbar expands; otherwise the ink row scrolls to the colour swatches and briefly highlights them, or, at compact width, the ink panel opens. Tool choices and Pencil gestures apply only while the canvas admits them, and a choice made during a stroke takes effect when the stroke ends.

A fling through the notebook keeps UIKit's native speed; one that would carry past the last page has its deceleration aimed at the page's end above the home indicator. A new drag from that resting end, released at least 72 points past it and not already heading back, lifts the page over an end-of-notebook card on the stone beneath it, with a light haptic tick on hardware that has one. The card's "Export as PDF" is shown disabled and marked coming later; its "Add page" adds a page, reads "Paper limit reached" at twelve pages and is disabled while writing is paused. A shorter pull springs back, and a fling arriving at the end never reveals the card. Scrolling back up dismisses it once it has left view and the page is back at its resting end, so giving up the card's room never moves the page; during a stroke the room is kept until the pen lifts. When the whole notebook fits on screen the card stays below the last page until a page is added or zooming in lets it scroll away. VoiceOver users reach the card through the page's "End of notebook options" action, and the notebook menu keeps "Add page at end".

A page added where it can be seen, from the card, the menu or the automatic extension while writing near the end, clears in with a frosted-glass unfold: a Metal port of the MIT-licensed duo_unfold shader from Duo Open blurs and dims a picture of the blank page, clearing from its top edge to its foot in about 0.6 seconds. The frost lies in the paper beneath the canvas, so ink drawn meanwhile shows above it and writing, scrolling and zooming carry on while it plays; each frame is one GPU draw, and a device that cannot keep up ends it early. A page added out of sight, such as page 11 while page 5 is open, simply appears. Reduce Motion replaces the frost with a 0.15-second fade.

Planned features appear as disabled placeholders that do nothing yet: Text, Insert, Record audio and Bookmark in the toolbar's “Soon” group, and every planned feature, Notebooks, Search, Summarize, Export and Backup among them, in the notebook menu's “Coming later” list.

Notty runs in a single window. Its tool selection is stored with that window's scene and restored with it; a new scene starts from the most recently chosen tool. Toolbar placement, collapse state and recent colours are app-wide preferences. These preferences are stored apart from notebook content, so missing or damaged values reset to defaults without affecting any notebook. Read-only recovery viewers allocate no tool store and receive no Pencil gestures. Simulator builds accept ordinary pointer input; device builds keep Pencil drawing separate from finger pan and pinch gestures.

Ivory paper fills the editor, with a faint fibre texture beneath the ink. The paper continues above the first page; flat stone with a faint page edge shows only past the last page, beside a zoomed-out page, or above the paper when zoomed far out. The texture is rendered once per launch off the main thread and is not redrawn while writing, scrolling or zooming; until it is ready, and whenever Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast is enabled, a flat colour replaces it. Theme colours are proposals pending review on the device. The wordmark and note title use the bundled Berkeley Mono typewriter face; toolbar labels, menus and other controls keep the system font, and the titles fall back to the system monospaced design if the bundled face is unavailable.

## Recovery copies

After a save failure, preserve a recovery copy before returning to the committed notebook. Background flushing automatically attempts preservation for uncovered failed edits, including a save that fails during the flush. Manual and background callers share the same preservation task; covered edits do not produce duplicate copies. A background lease limits the time available and does not guarantee completion after suspension or termination. Recovery copies retain every pending completed drawing, its original stroke identities, edit timestamps, title and paper extent. A copy must cover the current pending edits before returning to the saved version; newer edits invalidate that permission. If preservation or reopening fails, current pending work remains unsaved and retained.

Recovery copies are stored locally in a separate directory and can be inspected even when the notebook cannot open. Receipt-first listing skips copies belonging to other notebooks before reading their drawing data. Matching damaged, unsupported, unsafe and temporarily unreadable copies are reported separately from receipts that cannot be attributed. Metadata inspection does not establish archive health. Loading a selected edit verifies that edit independently, so a healthy edit inside a partially damaged copy can remain available. Reopening saved work requires full-copy verification against the exact current pending states before releasing them. Copies remain retained; this study does not provide deletion or general archive export.

New recovery copies use schema 3: a small hash-bound manifest, shared individual stroke archives, and one binary reference file per edit. Each ordered reference contains a 16-byte stroke UUID and a 32-byte archive digest. Every original edit identity, timestamp, title, paper extent and recovery provenance is preserved. The bundle is self-contained and has no dependency on normal notebook assets. Existing schema-1 and schema-2 whole-drawing copies remain readable without rewriting them, including their original positive paper extents.

Recovery bounds are 32 edits per copy, 20,000 stroke identities per state, 128 MiB per individual archive and logical state, and 512 MiB total logical referenced data per copy. Repeated references count every time, even when their bytes are stored once. New copies allow one to twelve sections, a 4 KiB title and a 2 MiB manifest. Legacy whole-drawing formats retain their original byte accounting. These emergency limits are independent of normal notebook limits; a preserved state can be inspectable while too large to restore into an editable notebook. Serialization and storage limits do not establish peak memory usage.

Restoring an eligible selected edit creates a fresh pending edit with a new identity, current capture stamp and the notebook's current expected revision. Separate provenance records the original notebook, recovery copy, edit, stamp and source base revision. Later ordinary edits do not inherit that provenance. Restoration requires a healthy current session without unsaved work or an active tool. Native barriers and final controller retirement check for late drawing changes before replacement; newly discovered work aborts restoration and remains retained. A smaller paper extent replaces the canvas generation without resetting the save coordinator or history audit. A conflicting revision or failed save retains the restored pending edit and original copy.

The selected-notebook host uses distinct validated notebook and recovery roots and an exact compare-and-swap selection pointer. It publishes and verifies a fresh UUID notebook before changing selection. Failed creation or pointer publication retains the old selection and any unselected new notebook. Ambiguous storage is never treated as empty, unselected notes are never automatically promoted, and a selected missing notebook is never recreated as blank at the same identity. Invalid selection data requires explicit recovery rather than an implicit overwrite. This is a recovery escape, not an organized library browser.

A small capacity indicator appears at 9,000 current strokes or a current successful encoding of at least 60,397,978 bytes. It uses existing codec measurements without additional drawing serialization and does not change save admission.

## Storage and history

Individual stroke archives are shared across immutable edit history. New notebooks use schema 2: each completed edit retains its revision identity, sequence, original edit stamp and save time in a hash-linked delta. Deltas record changed stroke archives, deletions, ordering, title and paper extent. Unchanged title and extent values are omitted. Full checkpoints are published only when actual encoded event bytes fund their size, with one bootstrap checkpoint; every edit event remains retained for future recording links and replay. Replay itself is not available.

Notebook publication verifies hashes and schema versions, rejects stale revisions, and updates its head after assets and revision metadata are written. First creation publishes a complete notebook from a private transaction. Missing publication metadata in an existing notebook requires recovery and does not create a blank notebook. Failed writes clean up their stages; recovery maintenance removes validated internal temporary transactions under the writer lock. Unsafe or oversized abandoned transactions are retained without preventing healthy storage operations; notebook maintenance records their failures in diagnostics. Notebook-stage cleanup follows successful content validation and skips temporary-looking directories and links without touching their targets. Cleanup errors retain the note for another maintenance attempt. Once notebook-stage maintenance succeeds, consecutive successful saves avoid directory scans; notebook-stage cleanup failures remain eligible for retry after publication. Private transaction cleanup runs on open and after a failed save.

Normal bounds are 10,000 strokes, 10 MiB per stroke asset, 64 MiB aggregate current stroke data, and 2 MiB metadata. This increment requires iPadOS 27 for native stable PencilKit stroke identities. Twelve sections and the validation bounds are study limits.

Public opening verifies the selected head and reconstructs current content from the nearest hash-linked checkpoint and subsequent deltas. It verifies each unique current stroke archive once, while counting every reference toward the 64 MiB logical byte bound. Damage on this reconstruction path fails opening. Unselected events and generations never become committed content. Opening cost depends on current content and its replay tail.

The session checks the complete selected history through an opaque opening pin on a cancellable utility worker, outside the repository actor and writer lock. This audit replays every retained event, checks checkpoint equality and byte funding, verifies every unique historical archive, and applies each state's logical byte bounds. Damage outside the current path leaves current reading available and closes new editing admission. Accepted pending edits, active-tool end snapshots and late owner observations continue through the existing save queue; successful saves do not clear an unresolved integrity warning. An incomplete storage check remains distinct from detected damage.

Published events, checkpoints, migration records and legacy revisions remain immutable and retained for audit pins. Asset repair installs only validated bytes under their correct digest. Future deletion, compaction, restore or garbage collection must preserve pin lifetimes or cancel and settle their audits before removing reachable files. A repair concurrent with an audit can produce a stale damage result; a fresh check resolves that race.

Existing schema-1 notebooks migrate side by side before their next normal save, or through an explicit repository migration call. Migration checks every selected legacy state against the new history before atomically switching the head, retaining original revisions and archives. Legacy parent metadata lacked digest links; migration validates the available chain and cannot retroactively establish that earlier parent metadata was unchanged. The selected migration receipt binds exact preserved head bytes, the migrated anchor and a fingerprint of every original selected revision. Opening validates the receipt record, preserved head metadata and anchor event through bounded metadata reads. The full audit checks the anchor's selected-chain membership and revision count. Rollback revalidates the retained legacy bytes and archives and is allowed only at the exact migration anchor, before any new schema-2 edit is accepted. A successful migration followed by a normal save failure can leave the equivalent migrated anchor selected without accepting the failed edit.

The native codec owns immutable copies of stroke archives and their computed digests. Unchanged strokes reuse these archives. Its normal comparison checks native path identity and drawing properties, including copied masks and render state; exact comparison also checks every supported point field. When a complete signature is unavailable, semantic barriers compare the original whole drawing through the SDK, and the codec reserializes affected strokes. These fallback costs are counted separately. Native path identity relies on PencilKit's supported immutable-path semantics. Public archives without a validated handle are copied and hashed before publication.

A transaction publishes an ordered prefix of at most eight edits beneath one head. Every accepted edit retains its own event and original stamp. A content rejection acknowledges preceding valid edits; a transaction failure before head publication acknowledges none. A 64 MiB budget limits unique newly introduced or repaired bytes selected for publication; it does not bound peak memory. The save coordinator checks receipt identities, ordering and rejection position before releasing pending work. Every observed native drawing retains its own generation, including callbacks arriving during a save and conservative end, flush and teardown barriers.

One additional owned Undo or Redo command is admitted during a capacity episode, before its native mutation. Queries and recovery presentation do not reset that reservation; genuine reopened admission does. PencilKit can invoke its raw undo manager through supported controls outside this owned command route, so this is not a global queue or memory bound. Every resulting actual drawing observation remains retained. Conditional paper extension is a separate edit using the triggering request’s stamp and current drawing. It waits for capacity and inactivity, rechecks ownership and paper state, and is cancelled on replacement, interruption or failure.

Warm saves reuse a previously verified generation only when every head field and its exact encoded bytes still match. They do not reread unchanged assets. This receipt records prior validation and does not establish the assets' present disk contents. Opening, explicit current-head audits, verification errors and observed foreign head changes refresh current verification. Successful same-head summary reads, historical loading and background audits preserve the prior current-state receipt; historical target verification does not mint a new current-state receipt. Candidate-backed repair remains restricted to regular files with validated replacement bytes; links and directories remain refused. Candidate-free loading rejects damaged assets in the requested current or historical state; full auditing rejects damaged assets anywhere in the selected pinned history.

The repository exposes metadata-only paginated history summaries, targeted historical state loading, a full pinned integrity audit and cumulative work counters. Summary pages verify hash-linked selected metadata and reconstruct the requested counts, title and extent without reading archives. A complete-head and verification-epoch cache records prior metadata validation; it does not establish present disk contents. Historical loading checks selected membership, reconstructs the target from its checkpoint and verifies only that state's archives. For owned native archive handles, ordinary append archive-byte work is bounded independently of existing stroke count. Public archives without those handles still require copying and hashing the supplied bytes. Input identity and reference checks still grow with current content, and byte-funded full checkpoints produce larger bursts. Counters expose these costs separately from elapsed-time diagnostics. SHA-256 formatting uses a compatible lookup-table hexadecimal encoder. This study is not a daily-use release.

File synchronization and atomic publication target process-crash recovery. Physical interruption, file protection, latency, thermal behavior, and abrupt power-loss durability remain acceptance checks.

Library organization, recording, PDF annotation, bookmarks, and export remain subsequent checkpoints.

## Cloud checks

The normal suite contains 347 core tests and 475 hosted iPad tests. A manual storage-stress check adds the extended 2,000-edit history case. A separate recovery-stress check preserves eight valid native drawing states of approximately 60 MiB each, verifies every retained state and measures all published files against the unique asset baseline. Native tests cover archive reuse and supported drawing-state changes; a hosted sustained-writing fixture schedules 60 drawing offers at four per second on a 2,000-stroke note, records every actual SDK observation and verifies its retained history. The Native checks workflow runs geometry, persistence/fault, recovery, and native PencilKit archive tests on macOS 27. It installs Xcode's Metal Toolchain, compiles unsigned iPad Simulator and iPad device applications using Xcode 27 and runs hosted notebook session, canvas and lifecycle tests in an iPadOS 27 Simulator.

The standard GitHub image is currently a preview; the workflow verifies actual host, toolchain and SDK versions and selects an available iPadOS 27.0 tablet simulator. A single job has a twenty-minute limit and cancels superseded checks on the same pull request. Hosted tests disable automatic simulator diagnostic collection and have bounded execution time. Failed test summaries, logs, result bundles and numeric storage measurements are uploaded with seven-day retention.

No Apple membership or signing credentials are needed for these cloud checks. Personal iPad previews use local Xcode and a free Personal Team when requested; TestFlight and external distribution remain deferred.

Cloud tests establish only their declared cases. They do not establish physical Pencil latency, freehand identity and callback behavior beyond these fixtures, device suspension or power-loss recovery.

## Building

Building requires Xcode 27 with the iOS 27 SDK and Xcode's Metal Toolchain component, which compiles the page unfold shader. Install the toolchain once per Xcode with `xcodebuild -downloadComponent MetalToolchain` (about 840 MB; no administrator rights needed); `xcodebuild -showComponent MetalToolchain` reports whether it is installed. The Native checks workflow installs it before compiling.

## Source layout

- `Notty/App`: application entry point and configuration.
- `Notty/Features/Notebook`: native editor and recovery inspection.
- `Notty/Resources`: bundled typefaces and the launch background colour.
- `NottyTests`: hosted iPad notebook and lifecycle tests.
- `Packages/NottyCore`: paper geometry, editor tool and toolbar models, versioned storage, recovery models/store, stroke codec, FIFO save coordination, and tests.
- `Notty.xcodeproj`: shared application project and scheme.
- `.github/workflows`: cloud validation.

The repository is the source of truth. Local application checkout and builds require explicit authorization.

## Product direction

The first usable release will add an organized local library, PDF annotation, built-in microphone recording, linked bookmarks, and PDF/archive export. Requested on-device AI and private AWS backup/restore follow. Multi-device synchronization and collaboration are outside the product scope.
